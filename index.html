Medical sociology · Population health · Aging and Life course · Chronic pain

I am a medical sociologist and social demographer and a postdoctoral fellow at the Seattle Children’s Research Institute. I earned my Ph.D. in Sociology from the University at Buffalo.

I have two major, interconnected research agendas. The first takes a life course perspective to understand how early-life experiences (e.g., stress exposure in utero, wartime trauma, childhoood poverty, lifetime stress) affect later health. I'm especially interested in understanding through what mechanisms early-life experiences affect later health outcomes, when resilience may occur; and how individual and institutional resources can promote resilience.My second research agenda focuses on contextualizing health disparities. I examine how broader sociopolitical and cultural contexts affect population health and health disparities across states and countries, and how different levels of social contexts interactively shape population health.

My methodological expertise includes multilevel and longitudinal modeling, categorical data analysis, spatial analysis, mediation and decomposition techniques, and causal inference methods (e.g., instrumental variables and difference-in-differences). My research has appeared in journals including the Journal of Health and Social Behavior, PAIN, American Journal of Public Health, Journal of Pain, etc.
