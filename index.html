Medical sociology · Population health · Aging and Life course · Chronic pain

I am a medical sociologist and social demographer and a postdoctoral fellow at the Seattle Children’s Research Institute. I earned my Ph.D. in Sociology from the University at Buffalo.

I have two major, interconnected research agendas. The first takes a life course perspective to understand how early-life adversities, such as childhood socioeconomic disadvantage, exposure to war, and cumulative stress, shape health and health inequalities in adulthood and later life. I'm especially interested in understanding how and through what mechanisms these early experiences affect later health outcomes, when the resilience may occur, and what individual and institutional resources can promote resilience.

My second research agenda focuses on understanding how broader sociopolitical and cultural contexts shape population health and health disparities across states and countries, and how different levels of social contexts interactively affect population health. My research has appeared in journals including the Journal of Health and Social Behavior, Journal of Pain, and PAIN.
