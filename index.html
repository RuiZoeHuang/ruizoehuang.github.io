Medical sociology · Population health · Aging and Life course · Chronic pain

I am a medical sociologist and social demographer and a postdoctoral fellow at the Seattle Children’s Research Institute. I earned her Ph.D. in Sociology from the University at Buffalo. My research examines how social structures and policy contexts shape health inequalities over the life course, with a particular focus on chronic pain. Drawing on longitudinal and comparative data from the United States, Europe, and Asia, I also investigate how different early-life expriences (e.g., wartime trauma,  childhood SES and lifetime cumulative stress) shape later-life pain and how individual resources, social relationships, and welfare institutions generate risk or support resilience. My current work extends this agenda to maternal stress, family protective factors, and neighborhood influences on pediatric pain. My research has appeared in journals including the Journal of Health and Social Behavior, Journal of Pain, and PAIN.
