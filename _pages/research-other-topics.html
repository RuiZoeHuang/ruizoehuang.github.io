Racial-Ethnic Differences in Healthcare Experience Among Middle-Aged and Older Adults in the United States: Evidence From Four Waves of the Health and Retirement Study ↗

Journal of Aging and Health

Data and Method:

  • Health and Retirement Study, 2014–2020: 16,088 respondents across four waves, with measures of satisfaction, affordability, access, and whether care reflected patients’ preferences.
  • Survey-weighted multilevel logistic regression and interactions with Medicaid and Medicare coverage.

Highlights:

  • Racial and ethnic disparities differed across dimensions of healthcare experience, rather than following one uniform pattern.
  • Unmet care preferences remained more common among Black, Hispanic, and other minoritized respondents than among non-Hispanic White respondents.
  • Medicaid reduced some gaps, especially for Hispanic respondents; Medicare’s moderating role was more limited.
