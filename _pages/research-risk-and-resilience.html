How early-life adversity shapes later-life health through risk and resilience processes.

Early-Life War Exposure and Later-Life Chronic Pain in Vietnam: Risk, Resilience, and Timing ↗

Journal of Health and Social Behavior

Data and Method:

  • 2018 Vietnam Health and Aging Study: 1,826 older adults with retrospective information about wartime experiences.
  • Regression and Karlson–Holm–Breen mediation analyses; comparisons across cohorts exposed in childhood, adolescence, or young adulthood.

Highlights:

  • Early-life war exposure was associated with greater risk of moderate or severe pain decades later.
  • War-related physical and mental health problems accounted for much of the association, alongside protective processes such as social engagement.
  • Those exposed as children were more vulnerable to wartime violence than the older exposure cohorts.
